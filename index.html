The Origin

One man.
One final
wish.

In 1986, Enzo Ferrari then 88 years old walked into his engineering team and said it simply: "Build me a car."

No brief. No specification deck. No market research. He meant the best car in the world. The car to celebrate 40 years of Ferrari. The car that would carry his name after he was gone.

He died in August 1988. The F40 was the last thing he signed off on.

"Build a car to be the best in the world. And now the car is here."

— Enzo Ferrari · July 21, 1987

The Legacy

Some things
don't need a sequel.

The F40 was never replaced. Ferrari didn't need to replace it. You don't improve on a farewell. You preserve it.

The 1,311 examples that exist are scattered across private collections, museums, and the occasional garage where someone still dares to drive it. Values have crossed $2 million at auction not because of hype, but because the world understands what it's looking at.

The F40 taught a generation of designers that restraint is the luxury. That removing things is harder than adding them. That the best brief you can give an engineer is a deadline and a dream.

Enzo knew he was dying. He built it anyway.
